# About

## The Story Behind Tiddy Hunter

You might be wondering where the name Tiddy Hunter came from.

The story goes back to 2021, when I started seeing Mexican Libertads being posted in the WallStreetSilver community on Reddit.  One collector in particular, known as Bullion Cat, regularly shared pictures of these beautiful coins. In the comments, people affectionately referred to Libertads as “Libertiddys.”

Somewhere along the way, that nickname inspired my own moniker: Tiddy Hunter, a hunter of Libertiddys.

What began as a bit of humor became my identity across social media, including Instagram, Facebook, and other collecting communities.  My YouTube channel came much later, as another way to share my research, observations, and passion for Mexican Libertads with a broader audience.

Bullion Cat and I eventually became friends and fellow collectors.  Along the way, I’ve also had the pleasure of meeting some genuinely wonderful people in the collecting community, people who share a passion for the hobby, enjoy exchanging knowledge, and appreciate what makes these coins special.

Of course, like any collectibles market, the Libertad community has its share of questionable characters, exaggerated claims, and individuals whose interests don’t always align with those of everyday collectors. Those experiences have only reinforced my belief in independent research, transparency, and giving collectors the information they need to make their own decisions.

## My Background

What began as a personal interest in silver during the aftermath of the 2008 financial crisis gradually evolved into a deeper fascination with monetary systems, inflation, market structure, and eventually the world of Mexican Libertads and modern numismatics.

Over time, I became increasingly drawn to areas of the market where historical context, scarcity, and data were often poorly documented or overlooked entirely. That interest ultimately led me toward researching Libertads, particularly low mintage and underappreciated modern Mexican issues.

## Why Libertads

For many years, I was primarily a stacker of world bullion coins. Libertads stood apart.

Unlike many modern bullion programs, the Libertad series developed a unique combination of:

* Limited mintages
* Irregular production
* Collector driven scarcity
* Historical inconsistency in documentation
* Unusually strong long term collector demand

As I spent more time studying the series, I realized that many aspects of the market lacked centralized research, particularly regarding:

* Surviving population estimates
* Grading population trends
* Historical availability
* Mintage inconsistencies
* Long term collector behavior

This archive was created to help document and preserve that information.

## Research Philosophy

I’ve always believed that collectors benefit from having access to information rather than relying solely on what dealers, sellers, or other market participants tell them.

Data can be incredibly powerful, but it can also be selectively presented to support a particular narrative.  A handful of carefully chosen transactions, an asking price mistaken for a market value, or a population report taken out of historical context can create a misleading picture.

My approach is to examine the broader evidence, identify limitations, question assumptions, and make the underlying analysis as transparent as possible.

That philosophy is also what inspired Libertad Value Track, an interactive research platform I’m developing to help collectors explore historical reported eBay sold prices, monthly price distributions, and market trends across different years, finishes, grading companies, and grades.

The goal is to make meaningful market information freely accessible, so collectors can evaluate the evidence and draw their own conclusions.

You don’t have to agree with my interpretation of the data. I’d rather give you the tools to examine it yourself.
